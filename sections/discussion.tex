\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The experiments separate a positive mechanism result from a negative system
result. Original-grid support improves over global, flattened, and
degree-matched controls, so the spatial prior is useful within this
architecture. The complete model nevertheless remains below dense DeiT-S/8
and ToMe in recorded top-1, and it is slower at 224 pixels in the available
random-weight benchmark. The current evidence therefore supports
``trainable spatial routing with a physical bottleneck,'' not a
state-of-the-art accuracy--efficiency frontier.

The design also retains substantial full-grid work. Soft transport does not
shorten the local stage; recovery reads all local features; and sparse scoring
still incurs indexing and initialization costs. A local edge constraint does
not bound the final receptive field after multiple transports, nor does it
establish semantic boundary preservation. The 150-epoch ImageNet geometry
study has one seed, while the replicated degree-matched evidence is on
CIFAR-100. Finally, validation-set checkpoint selection makes reported best
scores selected rather than independent estimates.

Two collaborator-side artifacts remain. One numeric packet fills the matched
DTEM, latency, memory, mass-bias, and recovery cells under a single protocol.
One passive trace from the final checkpoint supplies routing visualizations
with checks that tracing leaves logits, top-$K$ indices, and mass conservation
unchanged. No checkpoint transfer is required. Until those artifacts arrive,
the placeholders stay visibly unfilled and make no contribution to the
reported results.

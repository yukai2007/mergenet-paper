\section{Introduction}

Fine patch grids retain object boundaries and small visual details, whereas
global self-attention lets a vision transformer integrate evidence over the
whole image. The same token sequence that provides this flexibility also
drives the quadratic attention cost. Token pruning discards part of that
sequence; token merging instead consolidates redundant patches so that their
information can survive in fewer carriers~\cite{tome2023}. A learned merger
must solve two coupled problems: decide where information should flow, and
turn those decisions into the shorter tensor that later layers actually
process.

Continuous relaxations make the first problem trainable. DTEM learns
dedicated merge embeddings through soft grouping and soft merging
\cite{dtem2024}. Its continuous operators retain the token count, so its
end-to-end protocol alternates relaxed updates of the merge embeddings with
hard, token-reducing updates of the backbone. This leaves an architectural
question: can a model learn a continuous routing policy while the same
forward path exposes downstream blocks to the exact fixed token budget used
at inference?

We answer this question with \mn{}, a local-to-latent vision transformer.
The local encoder preserves the full patch grid while a sequence of soft
transport steps moves feature mass between slots. A fixed-budget gather then
selects the highest-mass carriers and physically changes the tensor shape from
$N$ patches to $K$ patches. A residual cross-attention read recovers evidence
from the final full-grid local features, and a latent encoder performs global
reasoning only on the $K$ carriers. Thus differentiable routing determines
the information flow, while an explicit compression boundary determines the
executed sequence length.

The routing graph injects a deliberately weak spatial prior. A donor may
route only to receiver positions within radius $R$ on the original
two-dimensional patch grid, but the network remains free to choose the edge
weights, accumulated mass, and final carrier locations inside that support.
The prior therefore rules out geometrically implausible long jumps without
prescribing a fixed pooling region. We test this design against global
routing, a row-major flattened window, and a receiver-permuted graph that
preserves the realized degree statistics.

Two implementation details make the compression trainable and compatible
with efficient attention. First, a threshold-adjusted soft top-$k$ operator,
following the construction described by Su~\cite{sujianlin2024topk}, replaces
serial selection with a parallel continuous mask. At the final bottleneck,
hard indices execute the exact budget and a straight-through carrier weight
returns gradients to the continuous scores; the method does not treat the
discrete indices themselves as differentiable. Second, each selected carrier
keeps its accumulated mass. Adding $\log m_j$ to the key logit gives
mass-aware proportional attention. A rank-one query/key augmentation can
encode this bias inside a standard FlashAttention call~\cite{flashattn2022}.

The empirical evidence supports the routing mechanism while defining the
remaining deployment measurements. On ImageNet-1K, radius-3 routing improves
150-epoch top-1 by $1.670\pp$ over global routing and $0.598\pp$ over a
flattened-window control. Across three CIFAR-100 seeds, radius 3 beats global,
flat, and degree-matched controls. The
complete 300-epoch model reaches 81.368\% top-1 at
224 pixels and a $2\times$ carrier reduction, compared with 82.246\% for
dense DeiT-S/8. A matched-budget re-evaluation records 81.360\% for \mn{} and
81.932\% for ToMe. We leave the matched DTEM, latency, and memory cells empty
until they are measured from the collaborator-held final checkpoints under
one protocol.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a differentiable-to-discrete compression boundary that learns soft
  spatial mass transport while making the latent encoder train and execute on
  an exact, input-dependent carrier budget;
  \item an original-grid spatial routing prior that constrains admissible
  transport but leaves similarities and carrier locations learned, supported
  by ImageNet controls and a three-seed, degree-matched CIFAR study; and
  \item a mass-aware attention formulation together with an exact rank-one
  FlashAttention-compatible implementation, plus an evaluation that separates
  routing accuracy, token count, arithmetic cost, and measured latency.
\end{itemize}

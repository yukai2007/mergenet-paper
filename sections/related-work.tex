\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Token reduction methods differ in which tokens they retain, how they aggregate
information, and where they reduce computation. DynamicViT learns token
retention decisions and trains them using differentiable attention
masking~\cite{dynamicvit2021}, while A-ViT assigns adaptive computation depth
through token halting~\cite{avit2022}. Token Merging (ToMe) combines similar
tokens through bipartite soft matching and can be applied to an existing
checkpoint without additional training or incorporated into
training~\cite{tome2023}. Subsequent work changes the merge criterion:
PiToMe protects informative or isolated tokens using an energy
score~\cite{pitome2024}; MCTF combines similarity, informativeness, and token
size with one-step-ahead attention and reduction-consistency
training~\cite{mctf2024}; SAD-TM uses visual saliency and a deferred-merging
variant~\cite{sadtm2026}. These methods provide stronger comparisons for a
compression mechanism than an uncompressed backbone alone.

The closest methodological predecessor of MergeNet is Decoupled Token
Embedding for Merging (DTEM), which learns low-dimensional merge embeddings
through continuously relaxed grouping and merging~\cite{dtem2024}. DTEM
separates the representation used to select merges from the features used by
the backbone. Its soft embedding updates retain the physical token count;
end-to-end training alternates these updates with backbone updates that use
hard merging, and inference uses bipartite matching. MergeNet builds on this
lineage through the OpenToMe implementation~\cite{opentome}. Our study concerns
the support of the routing operator: which donor--receiver pairs are eligible
under a prescribed carrier budget. The decoupled embedding and soft merge
operators are inherited components. The unconstrained MergeNet variant is an
internal architectural control and should not be interpreted as a reproduction
of the original DTEM training and inference protocol.

Spatial locality is also an established design choice for token reduction.
DSM delays merging, applies it within spatial windows, and then transitions
to global merging~\cite{dsm2024}. ALGM similarly combines early local merging
with later global merging for semantic segmentation~\cite{algm2024}.
GTP-ViT propagates tokens over a graph that combines adjacency on the original
image grid with semantic connections~\cite{gtpvit2024}. More recent spatial
reducers impose different constraints: CubistMerge restricts matching to
neighbors along rows and columns while preserving a rectangular token
layout~\cite{cubistmerge2026}, whereas MAT merges adjacent tokens after
Hilbert-curve reordering~\cite{napmat2025}. MergeNet instead defines eligible
routes by a Euclidean radius on the original patch grid. This distinction
motivates controlled comparisons of global, flattened-window, and
two-dimensional routing support; it does not establish priority for locality
or for hard spatial constraints.

Compression and recovery must also be distinguished from the routing rule.
LookupViT processes a fixed set of compressed tokens alongside a cheaper
high-resolution token stream, exchanging information through bidirectional
cross-attention~\cite{lookupvit2024}. ALGM restores token resolution for dense
prediction~\cite{algm2024}, while DToP uses early predictions for confident
segmentation tokens and combines predictions across stages without
reconstructing those tokens~\cite{dtop2023}. These methods show that retaining
or recovering dense information is a separate architectural choice. Our
classification study evaluates the behavior and cost of a particular
local--latent architecture; it does not establish a new general recovery
mechanism or its suitability for dense prediction.

Evaluation protocols determine what a comparison can establish. ToMe includes
DeiT models trained from scratch for 300 epochs, whereas DTEM primarily
studies adaptation of pretrained models~\cite{tome2023,dtem2024}. Published
results also depend on the backbone and its training
recipe~\cite{deit2021}; they cannot substitute for controlled comparisons in
our patch-8 setting. DiffRate further demonstrates that layerwise compression
rates are a design variable distinct from the merge
criterion~\cite{diffrate2023}. Equal final token counts therefore do not imply
equal computation throughout the network. Moreover, DSM reports that data
movement associated with local merging can limit throughput gains despite
arithmetic savings~\cite{dsm2024}. We accordingly separate the internal
geometry study from comparisons with post-training ToMe and PiToMe, and
distinguish achieved token counts, arithmetic cost, and measured latency.
The resulting scope is a study of spatial routing and its execution costs,
with conclusions bounded by the evaluated models, training protocols, and
hardware.

\section{Synthetic Efficiency}
\label{app:followup}

We measure inference on one idle A100-SXM4-80GB with randomly
initialized models, device-resident $224\times224$ inputs, and fp16
autocast. Each method and batch size runs in a fresh process with
50 warmups followed by five rounds of 200 CUDA-event samples.
The reported latency is the median of all 1,000 samples; timing
excludes model setup, data loading, and diagnostic hooks. Dense
DeiT-S/8 has 784 patch tokens, while each compressed method reaches
392 final patches, excluding CLS. ToMe and PiToMe both enable
proportional attention.

Table~\ref{tab:a100-efficiency} reports the eager measurements.
The five-round median coefficient of variation is below 0.3\%
for every batch-64 cell. Batch-1 measurements are more sensitive
to host scheduling: two independent \mn{} runs yield
18.27 and 21.00\,ms, despite each passing the 3\% within-run
variation threshold.

As a separate implementation comparison, we compile ToMe's transformer
block forwards. For \mn{}, we compile local and latent transformer
blocks, cross-attention, and selection and merge operations, while
leaving the enclosing local-encoder forward eager.
At batch 64, their latencies are 27.22 and 27.24\,ms, respectively;
peak allocated memory is 0.614 and 0.627\,GiB. At batch 1, ToMe
takes 9.24\,ms and \mn{} takes 11.22\,ms. The compiled and eager
measurements are separate operating points. All A100 measurements
use random weights and cannot form an accuracy--latency comparison
with trained checkpoints. The current ToMe and PiToMe adapters also
do not use the fixed upstream commits required for the final
common-protocol evaluation (Appendix~\ref{app:pending-evaluations}).

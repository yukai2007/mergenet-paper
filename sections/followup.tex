\section{A100 Timing}
\label{app:followup}

An independent A100-SXM4-80GB experiment compares dense DeiT-S/8,
ToMe, and the sparse-backend \mn{} with randomly initialized weights.
All cells use batch 64, fp16 autocast, and 392 final patches at 224 pixels
or 1,152 at 384 pixels for compressed methods. The measured forward
pass excludes data loading, training, and diagnostic hooks. ToMe's
proportional-attention setting is shown explicitly; its enabled setting
matches the accuracy evaluation. The BNHD rows use a local-attention
tensor-layout change, rather than a different trained checkpoint.

Each cell uses ten warmups and thirty CUDA-event samples in each of
three rounds. Table~\ref{tab:followup-latency} reports the median of
the three round medians.

On this hardware and software stack, the original \mn{} is slower
than dense DeiT at both resolutions. BNHD reduces full-model
radius-3 time by about 5\%. ToMe's proportional-attention setting
also changes latency substantially. The A100 ordering differs from
the 384-pixel H20-3e radius-3 result; the two experiments differ in
hardware and software, so neither establishes an accelerator-independent
speed ranking. These random-weight times are kept separate from the
trained-checkpoint accuracy and common-protocol benchmark.

\subsection{Efficiency}
\label{sec:systems}

The routing gains must be considered alongside the work required to
produce the shorter sequence. \mn{} reduces the latent-stage input
from 784 to 392 patches, but its six local blocks still process the
full grid and recovery reads those features again. Accounting for
these operations gives 14.13 GMAC per image at 224 pixels, compared
with 22.41 GMAC for dense DeiT-S/8, a 36.9\% reduction. The
estimate excludes indexing and data movement, so it does not
predict inference latency by itself.

Existing synthetic benchmarks show why the measured implementation
matters. On an H20-3e at batch 64, random-weight \mn{} takes
45.71 ms versus 42.68 ms for dense DeiT-S/8 at 224 pixels, while
the 384-pixel comparison is 136.54 versus 170.61 ms. An
independent A100 benchmark does not reproduce the latter ordering
on its software stack (Appendices~\ref{app:synthetic}
and \ref{app:followup}). These are architecture-only measurements
with random weights. The 384-pixel H20 ToMe timing also disables proportional
attention, unlike its accuracy evaluation. They therefore cannot
be paired with checkpoint accuracy. Under the
common-protocol trained-checkpoint evaluation, \mn{} records
\pendingvalue{} ms batch-1 latency, \pendingvalue{} images/s batch-64
throughput, and \pendingvalue{} GiB peak allocated memory. The comparison
uses the selected checkpoints under a common GPU and precision,
with each method's accuracy-evaluation attention setting and
achieved token counts recorded
(Appendix~\ref{app:pending-evaluations}).

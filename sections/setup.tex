\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate \mn{} on ImageNet-1K, study its accuracy across carrier budgets
and image resolutions, and measure the cost of its implementation.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data and training.}
We train on ImageNet-1K and report top-1 accuracy on its 50,000-image
validation set using exponential moving average (EMA) weights.
At $224\times224$ resolution, DeiT-S/8 starts from 784 patch tokens.
Dense DeiT and \mn{} train from scratch for 300 epochs with AdamW,
an effective global batch of 1,024, cosine learning-rate decay,
five warmup epochs, and the same augmentation family. The learning rate
is $5\times10^{-4}$ for DeiT and $7.5\times10^{-4}$ for \mn{}.
The 384-pixel models fine-tune the corresponding 224-pixel EMA weights
for 30 epochs; the 512-pixel models receive a further 15 epochs.
Appendix~\ref{app:recipe} gives the training settings.

\paragraph{Models and token budgets.}
\mn{} uses six local blocks followed by six latent blocks and gathers
392 patch carriers at its default $2\times$ compression setting.
We compare it with dense DeiT-S/8 and with ToMe and PiToMe applied to the
same trained dense checkpoint at inference. The compressed methods are
matched by final patch count, excluding CLS. Their compression schedules
remain different: ToMe and PiToMe merge across blocks, while \mn{}
gathers between the local and latent stages. Dense DeiT-S/16 provides
an additional coarse-patch reference with 196 patches.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
The main compressed-model comparisons use complete validation evaluations
of selected EMA checkpoints. Training-duration and resolution results
report the best EMA top-1 recorded during each training stage. Carrier-budget
sweeps evaluate fixed checkpoints without retraining for each budget.
Latency and memory are measured separately on synthetic inputs with random
weights, using fp16 autocast and device-resident tensors.
Section~\ref{sec:systems} reports these implementation benchmarks.

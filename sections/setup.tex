\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

We evaluate \mn{} on ImageNet-1K classification, then compare
routing variants to study the spatial prior. We also examine how accuracy
changes with the carrier budget and image resolution, and distinguish
theoretical savings from measured inference time.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data and training.}
Our main experiments train on ImageNet-1K and report EMA top-1 accuracy on
all 50,000 validation images. At $224\times224$ resolution, the DeiT-S/8
backbone~\citep{deit2021} starts from 784 patch tokens. Dense DeiT-S/8
and \mn{} train from scratch for 300 epochs with AdamW, an effective
global batch of 1,024, cosine
learning-rate decay, five warmup epochs, and the same augmentation
family. The selected \mn{} run uses a learning rate of
$7.5\times10^{-4}$; dense DeiT uses $5\times10^{-4}$. The spatial-routing
controls use the latter rate for 150 epochs. Appendix~\ref{app:recipe}
gives the full recipes and run inventory.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
\mn{} gathers 392 patch carriers after six full-grid local blocks.
ToMe and PiToMe instead compress the same trained dense checkpoint at
inference time. We set all three methods to 392 final patches, excluding
CLS, while keeping their different compression schedules explicit.
The patch-8 DTEM adaptation follows the common recipe
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). For the resolution study, 384-pixel models
fine-tune the 224-pixel EMA weights for 30 epochs, and 512-pixel models
receive a further 15 epochs.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
The compressed rows use full-validation evaluations of selected EMA
checkpoints; the dense score is the best EMA training-summary endpoint.
In particular, \mn{} scores 81.360\% at 392 patches in the
re-evaluation, compared with 81.368\% for its best training-summary
checkpoint. We keep these records distinct. The reported values are
checked against training summaries and supplied evaluation records.
The trained-checkpoint runtime comparison uses a common GPU, precision,
and batch protocol, with each method's accuracy-evaluation attention
setting; existing synthetic benchmarks are reported
separately in Section~\ref{sec:systems}.

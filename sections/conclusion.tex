\section{Conclusion}

Spatially restricting soft token routing improves the recorded accuracy of
\mn{} relative to its global and flattened-window controls. The ImageNet
benefit survives both best- and final-checkpoint reporting. A three-seed
CIFAR-100 campaign reproduces a radius-3 advantage under two selectors and
against a degree-matched support control. Higher-resolution fine-tuning
narrows the ImageNet gap to dense DeiT-S/8 at 384 pixels, but a completed
512-pixel stage does not continue that closing. Completed optimizer probes
do not exceed the campaign's improvement threshold over the selected
150-epoch configuration. The composed system
still does not establish a superior compression method. Full-grid processing
before the gather, recovery cost, and routing overhead separate nominal
compression from execution savings. The measured dense-relative latency
balance improves at higher resolution on H20, while the independent A100
stack shows no dense-relative speedup at either tested resolution. A unified
local timing protocol and CIFAR checkpoint replay narrow the evidence: the
layout change gives modest whole-model gains, and spatial-routing accuracy
benefits persist despite observable batch dependence. Strong post-training
accuracy baselines and unavailable ImageNet weights still limit deployment
claims. The resulting study provides a reproducible account of the measured
tradeoffs, a three-seed routing confirmation, and a specific batch-dependence
diagnostic, while leaving a corrected, checkpoint-verified
accuracy--latency frontier and the interrupted representation-initialization
run as explicit open results.

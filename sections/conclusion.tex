\section{Conclusion}

Spatially restricting soft token routing improves the recorded ImageNet
accuracy of \mn{} relative to its global and flattened-window controls.
The benefit survives both best- and final-checkpoint reporting, and
higher-resolution fine-tuning narrows the gap to dense DeiT-S/8. It does not,
however, establish a superior compression system. Full-grid processing before
the gather, recovery cost, and routing overhead separate nominal compression
from execution savings. The measured dense-relative latency balance improves
at higher resolution, but strong post-training accuracy baselines and an
attention-policy mismatch further limit deployment claims. The resulting
study provides a reproducible account of the measured tradeoffs and a
specific batch-dependence diagnostic, while leaving a corrected,
checkpoint-verified accuracy--latency frontier as an explicit open result.

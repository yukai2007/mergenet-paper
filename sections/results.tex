\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:primary-results}

\mn{} reaches 81.360\% top-1 at 224 pixels while passing 392 patch
carriers to its latent encoder (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Dense DeiT-S/8
reaches 82.246\%; its post-training ToMe and PiToMe variants reach
81.932\% and 81.742\% at the same final patch count as \mn{}.
These results place the learned physical bottleneck within 0.886
percentage points of the dense fine-patch model. The coarse-patch
DeiT-S/16 reference reaches 79.692\% with 196 patches; its smaller
input sequence is a distinct operating point.

Longer scratch training improves both dense and compressed models.
\mn{} increases from 79.316\% after 150 epochs to 81.368\% after
300 epochs, a gain of 2.052 percentage points in best EMA top-1.
Dense DeiT-S/8 increases from 79.992\% to 82.246\% under the
corresponding schedules. Appendix~\ref{app:training-budget} reports
these training-stage results, which are separate from the checkpoint
re-evaluations in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\subsection{Token Budget and Resolution}
\label{sec:budget-resolution}

Changing the carrier budget provides multiple inference operating points
from one trained model (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}). At 224 pixels,
increasing the final budget from 196 to 523 patches raises \mn{} top-1
from 80.228\% to 81.380\%. At 384 pixels, the fine-tuned model reaches
82.562\% with 1,152 carriers and 81.300\% with 576. The best measured
384-pixel point occurs at 1,152 carriers; retaining more carriers does
not necessarily improve a checkpoint trained at a fixed budget.
ToMe retains higher top-1 across the displayed budgets, while the
relative ordering of \mn{} and PiToMe varies with the operating point.
Appendix~\ref{app:latency} lists all budget evaluations.

Higher-resolution fine-tuning improves the trained classifier
(Table~\ref{tab:resolution}). At 384 pixels, \mn{} reaches 82.582\%
with $R=3$, compared with 82.976\% for dense DeiT-S/8.
The radius-scaled branch uses $R=3\times384/224\approx5.143$ and reaches
82.574\%; its checkpoint supplies the 384-pixel budget sweep.
Fine-tuning that branch at 512 pixels with $R\approx6.857$ reaches
82.618\%, compared with 83.068\% for dense DeiT-S/8. Table~\ref{tab:resolution}
therefore identifies the radius used by each reported model.

We also evaluate resolution changes without fine-tuning, using the
224-pixel checkpoints and unchanged radius $R=3$.
At the $2\times$ carrier budget, \mn{} reaches 81.400\% at 288 pixels
and 79.740\% at 384 pixels. The corresponding 384-pixel fine-tuned
checkpoint reaches 82.562\%, indicating that higher resolution benefits

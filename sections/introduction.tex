\section{Introduction}

Fine patch grids preserve spatial detail but increase the cost of vision
transformers. Token merging offers a way to retain information from that grid
while reducing the sequence processed by later layers~\cite{tome2023}.
Learning the merge criterion can make compression responsive to the task,
but a useful learned router must satisfy two distinct requirements: its
aggregation decisions must preserve useful representations, and its execution
must save enough work to pay for routing itself. An improvement in the first
requirement does not imply an improvement in the second.

We study this distinction through \mn{}, a classifier that combines a
DTEM-derived soft router~\cite{dtem2024} with a full-grid local encoder, a fixed
carrier budget, global recovery attention, and a compressed latent encoder.
The routing constraint is simple: a donor can send mass only to receiver slots
within a Euclidean radius on the original image grid. The constraint prevents
a flattened sequence from treating a row boundary as ordinary spatial
adjacency. It also limits candidate support independently of the learned
similarity. We compare this router with unconstrained and flattened-window
versions of the same architecture before comparing the complete model with
dense and post-training compression baselines.

The resulting ImageNet-1k campaign supports a qualified conclusion. With
matched 150-epoch settings, radius-3 routing improves best validation accuracy
by $1.670\pp$ over the unconstrained router and $0.598\pp$ over the flattened
window. These are observed single-seed improvements, rather than estimates
of a population effect. They do not establish that geometry alone explains
the benefit: the constraints also change candidate counts and the score
normalization context.

The deployment evidence is less favorable. At 224 pixels, a 300-epoch
\mn{} reaches 81.368\% top-1, compared with 82.246\% for dense DeiT-S/8.
Its batch-64 inference takes 45.71\,ms on a guarded H20 benchmark, compared
with 42.68\,ms for the dense model, despite lower estimated arithmetic cost.
After 384-pixel fine-tuning, the accuracy gap narrows to $0.394\pp$.
Post-training ToMe remains a strong accuracy baseline at matched final patch
counts. However, the available ToMe accuracy and latency harnesses use
different proportional-attention settings, so their outputs cannot be
combined into a verified accuracy--latency frontier.

Our contributions are an implementation-specific analysis of spatially
constrained soft routing, a traceable evaluation of its internal and external
comparisons, and a diagnosis of why nominal compression and realized savings
can diverge. We explicitly distinguish the support of a routing step from the
receptive field of a feature, and soft mass transfer from physical sequence
reduction. The study establishes useful internal controls and concrete
reproduction requirements; it does not claim state-of-the-art compression,
priority for spatial merging, or a general failure of learned token merging.

\begin{abstract}
Spatial constraints can improve learned token aggregation without making the
resulting model an efficient alternative to dense computation. We examine
this distinction in MergeNet, a DTEM-derived classifier that performs
original-grid-constrained soft mass transfer, selects a fixed number of
carriers, and recovers information before latent processing. In a
single-seed ImageNet-1k campaign, radius-3 routing improves 150-epoch top-1
accuracy by 1.670 percentage points over an unconstrained router and 0.598
points over a flattened-window control. An independent CIFAR-100 campaign
with three seeds, two selectors, and a degree-matched support control
reproduces a radius-3 advantage in every paired comparison, including
$+1.42\pm0.30$ points over receiver-permuted degree-matched support under the historical
selector. The full ImageNet model reaches 81.368\% after 300 epochs at 224
pixels, versus 82.246\% for dense DeiT-S/8. Fine-tuning narrows the gap to
82.582\% versus 82.976\% at 384 pixels, then 82.618\% versus 83.068\% at 512
pixels: the dense-relative deficit does not keep closing with resolution.
Four completed 150-epoch probes, including longer warmup, a
$\lambda$ curriculum, a higher learning rate, and matched stochastic depth,
remain inside a campaign-defined $\pm0.40$ point decision band around the selected
configuration. Despite 36.9\% fewer estimated multiply--accumulates, MergeNet
takes 7.1\% more inference time in a guarded 224-pixel synthetic H20
benchmark. A 384-pixel batch-64 radius-3 benchmark reduces inference time by
20.0\% relative to dense DeiT, while an independent A100 harness finds no
dense-relative speedup at either resolution on the local stack. Post-training
ToMe has higher recorded accuracy at all twenty matched final-token budgets.
The study separates internal routing gains from deployment evidence and
identifies the remaining representation-level checks that still require
collaborator-side checkpoints.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
Spatial constraints can improve learned token aggregation without making the
resulting model an efficient alternative to dense computation. We examine
this distinction in MergeNet, a DTEM-derived classifier that performs
original-grid-constrained soft mass transfer, selects a fixed number of
carriers, and recovers information before latent processing. In a
single-seed ImageNet-1k campaign, radius-3 routing improves 150-epoch top-1
accuracy by 1.670 percentage points over an unconstrained router and 0.598
points over a flattened-window control. The full model reaches 81.368\% after
300 epochs at 224 pixels, versus 82.246\% for dense DeiT-S/8; 384-pixel
fine-tuning narrows the gap to 82.582\% versus 82.976\%. Despite 36.9\% fewer
estimated multiply--accumulates, MergeNet takes 7.1\% more inference time in a
guarded 224-pixel synthetic H20 benchmark, while a 384-pixel batch-64 benchmark
on H20 reduces inference time by 20.0\%. An independent A100 harness
finds no dense-relative speedup at either resolution on the local stack. Post-training ToMe has higher recorded
accuracy at all twenty matched final-token budgets, although incompatible
attention settings prevent combining the available accuracy and latency
results into a verified joint frontier. Operator and local CIFAR checkpoint probes reveal batch-dependent soft
selection; the tested spatial-routing gain persists under rebatching. The study separates internal routing
gains from deployment evidence and identifies the implementation and
reproduction checks needed to assess learned compression reliably.
\end{abstract}
